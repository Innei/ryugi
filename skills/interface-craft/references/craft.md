# Interface craft — conditional heuristics

Use only the domains that matter for the particular design. These are tests of intent and coherence, not an imposed visual house style. The current project design system and brand are authoritative.

## Hierarchy and spacing

- Can a first-time visitor locate the primary task and next action? Is hierarchy defined by the task rather than decorative novelty?
- Use density suited to audience and duration: expert tools may require compact interfaces; reading-oriented products may need generous rhythm.
- Check alignment, grouping, consistent repeated dimensions and sensible nested spacing. A spacing scale is a default, not a ban on optical corrections.

## Typography and copy

- Choose roles, scale, weights and typefaces for readability, scripts/languages and product character.
- Align numerical columns, use consistent precision and label units/comparison periods.
- Prefer accurate, specific action language over vague hype or unsupported claims. Preserve deliberate product voice.

## Color, media and material

- Prioritize readable contrast and clear semantic state changes. Do not rely on hue alone.
- Real data, screenshots and domain imagery are often stronger than unrelated decoration.
- Gradients, glass, translucency, 3D, saturated palettes and rich art are valid when supported by platform, context or brand.
- Consider light/dark modes, reduced transparency and intentional focus.

## Components, states and motion

- Check relevant interaction states: focus, hover, press, disabled, loading, error, success, empty and selected. Do not invent unrelated states.
- Motion should indicate cause, space, state or feedback. Consider performance and reduced-motion preferences.
- A playable prototype should have functional visible actions; do not label an inert demo interactive.
- Inspect practical viewport sizes, text scaling, clipping, touch targets and accessibility.

## Distinctiveness without excess

Can a user describe what is special about the product from its actual content, interaction and wording? If swapping the product's name preserves the entire experience, revisit what it communicates before adding decoration. A signature may be omitted entirely.
